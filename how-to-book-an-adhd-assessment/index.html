ADHD Junction/How to book

FROM DECIDING TO ARRANGING

How to book an ADHD assessment.

There is no single UK booking system. The practical route depends on who the assessment is for, where you live and whether it is NHS-funded or self-funded.

01 · BEGIN HERE

First, identify the right starting point.

You do not need to arrive with a diagnosis already decided. The useful starting point is what has been happening, how long it has been happening and where it causes difficulty.

FOR AN ADULT

Start with a GP—or with careful private-provider checks.

For a local NHS assessment, arrange a GP appointment and explain the recurring difficulties and their effect on work, study, relationships or everyday life. The GP may then refer to an ADHD specialist service.

Many private services accept self-referrals, but check the clinician, assessment, report and arrangements after diagnosis before completing forms or paying.

Prepare for the first conversation →

FOR A CHILD OR YOUNG PERSON

School and healthcare may both be part of the route.

Speak to a teacher or SENCO and consider making a GP appointment. The local route may begin with either, and information from more than one setting is commonly important.

Support at home and school should continue while a referral or assessment is awaited. For a private service, confirm that it assesses the child’s age group and uses appropriately qualified child specialists.

Read the children and young people guide →
England, Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland?

Local NHS pathways differ across the UK. Right to Choose, as described below, is an NHS patient-choice route in England; different arrangements operate elsewhere.

02 · CHOOSE THE ROUTE

Three ways the booking can begin.

Still deciding? Compare the practical consequences before choosing. Waiting time is only one part of the decision.

01

NHS PATHWAY

Local NHS

  1. Find the local starting point.For adults this will commonly be a GP. For a child it may involve a GP, school or local children’s service.
  2. Describe the pattern and its impact.Bring a few ordinary examples, including what happens in different settings and any relevant childhood history.
  3. Ask where the referral is going.Record the service, date and how you will know it has been received. Ask what support is available while waiting.

There is no assessment fee, but eligibility, referral arrangements and waiting information vary locally.

02

NHS PATIENT CHOICE · ENGLAND

Right to Choose

  1. Check that the choice applies.If a GP considers a referral clinically appropriate, NHS choice rights may allow an eligible person in England to choose a qualifying service. Exceptions apply.
  2. Choose the precise service.Confirm the provider accepts the relevant age group and referral, and check whether assessment, medication, titration and continuing care are included.
  3. Take the current referral instructions to the GP.Ask how the request will be sent, retain any reference or copy, and confirm that the chosen service has received it.

Right to Choose is NHS-funded care. An independent organisation is not automatically an eligible provider for every ADHD referral.

03

SELF-FUNDED

Private assessment

  1. Shortlist the service, then examine the assessment.Check who will assess, their ADHD training and expertise, what information is gathered, and whether the process follows recognised guidance.
  2. Obtain the complete cost in writing.Include the assessment, report, follow-up, medication titration, prescriptions and ongoing reviews—not only the headline assessment fee.
  3. Book only after checking what follows.Understand cancellation terms, report times, treatment arrangements and what happens if a GP does not accept Shared Care.

A private diagnosis does not guarantee NHS prescriptions, GP Shared Care or acceptance by another service.

03 · BEFORE YOU COMMIT

A booking is the moment to ask—not assume.

A short written exchange before referral or payment can prevent much larger problems afterwards.

Open the full provider checklist →
  • 01

    Who will conduct and sign off the assessment? Check their profession, registration where applicable, and ADHD expertise.

  • 02

    What does the assessment include? It should be more than a questionnaire or computer test alone.

  • 03

    Is a written diagnostic report included? Ask when it is supplied and what happens if ADHD is not diagnosed.

  • 04

    What is the complete first-year cost? Include every likely fee after the assessment.

  • 05

    If medication is considered, who handles titration? Establish the likely start time, price and prescribing route.

  • 06

    What happens if Shared Care is declined? Ask whether specialist prescribing and reviews can continue, and at what cost.

  • 07

    What information will you need? Ask about school history, someone who knew you earlier, and information from another setting.

  • 08

    Can adjustments be made? Explain communication, sensory, mobility or appointment needs before booking.

04 · AFTER THE REQUEST

Confirm it. Record it. Then prepare.

Do not assume that sending a form or discussing a referral means you are on a waiting list. Ask the receiving service to confirm what it has received and what happens next.

1Keep the date

Record when the referral or booking was made and by whom.

2Confirm receipt

Ask whether anything is missing and when you should chase if you hear nothing.

3Check the next stage

Find out whether questionnaires, school information or an informant are required.

4Prepare without rehearsing

Collect real examples and practical questions rather than trying to produce a particular answer.
