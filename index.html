ADHD Assessment Checklist

Part A · Choosing your route and provider

Use this before you book. A worksheet for recording what you find while comparing routes and providers for ADHD assessment. It organises information. It does not assess symptoms or recommend a provider. Waits, prices and arrangements change often, so note the date beside anything you're told.

Your answers stay private

Queuend does not receive or see anything you enter in this worksheet. Your answers are not submitted to Queuend or stored on our servers.

If you save your progress, it is stored in this browser on this device so you can return to it later. Queuend does not upload that saved copy. If you download a backup, it is saved wherever your device normally puts downloaded files; that location may be synchronised to a service such as Google Drive, OneDrive or iCloud.

This is your personal record. You decide whether to keep it, delete it or share it with anyone.

Unless you choose to save your progress, your answers exist only on the page while it is open, and closing or reloading will lose them. Saved progress can be reopened by anyone using the same browser profile, and clearing browser data may remove it.

A backup copy is an ordinary file downloaded to your device, and it is not encrypted. If you share a device, keep it somewhere private or delete it when you no longer need it.

04

The GP conversation

Your GP may be central to the NHS and Right to Choose referral routes, and may later decide whether to accept Shared Care. Keep this separate from any single provider.

Why this matters Written reasons matter. They tell you exactly what to address, and they're what an ICB or a complaint would need to see.

If using Right to Choose England only

Watch out These are different answers with different consequences. Declining Shared Care with a particular provider later is lawful and fairly common. Refusing the referral itself has only narrow lawful grounds — clinical appropriateness, or the provider not holding a qualifying NHS contract for your service and age group. Practice preference is not one of them. If you are told you cannot be referred to a provider you have chosen, ask for the reason in writing. You are not expected to judge whether a contract legally qualifies; these questions exist so the answer is on the record rather than assumed.
