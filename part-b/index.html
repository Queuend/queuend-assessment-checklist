Preparing for your assessment

A worksheet for organising what you need to have ready, ask, take and remember, and for recording what you are told afterwards. Fill it in here, save your progress, and come back to it as often as you need. It is not a test, a symptom questionnaire, or a way of working out what the “right” answers are!

Your answers stay private

Queuend does not receive or see anything you enter in this worksheet. Your answers are not submitted to Queuend or stored on our servers.

If you save your progress, it is stored in this browser on this device so you can return to it later. Queuend does not upload that saved copy. If you download a backup, it is saved wherever your device normally puts downloaded files; that location may be synchronised to a service such as Google Drive, OneDrive or iCloud.

This is your personal record. You decide whether to keep it, delete it or share it with anyone.

Unless you choose to save your progress, your answers exist only on the page while it is open, and closing or reloading will lose them. Saved progress can be reopened by anyone using the same browser profile, and clearing browser data may remove it.

A backup copy is an ordinary file downloaded to your device, and it is not encrypted. If you share a device, keep it somewhere private or delete it when you no longer need it.

My appointment

Before the day
Already completed My ADHD Reflection Record? You do not need to write the same information out again. Keep it with you, or have it available for your assessment. Where something below is already covered there, simply tick Already covered in my Reflection Record and move on.

Haven’t used it? My ADHD Reflection Record is a separate free worksheet for organising what you have noticed about yourself. It opens in a new tab, so you will not lose your place here. It is optional, and the next three sections work without it.
02

What I want to remember about my experience

If you have completed the Reflection Record, this section should take a moment. It is short by design.

Which applies to me?
Why this matters Specific real-life examples are usually more useful to an assessor than trying to describe yourself in diagnostic language. If you have not used the Reflection Record, a few concrete moments written here — what happened, where, and what followed — will serve you better than a general description.
03

Earlier life and information I may have available

Assessments usually explore whether relevant patterns were present earlier in life. This section is about what you can supply, not about setting your history down again.

What I have
Has the provider specifically asked for childhood information or evidence?
Watch out Not having old paperwork should not automatically prevent an assessment. ADHD assessment explores whether relevant patterns were present earlier in life, but many adults no longer have school reports or other records. If you cannot provide something the service requested, tell them rather than trying to fill the gap yourself.

Questions I want to ask

Prompts, if useful When will I know the outcome? Will there be a feedback appointment? When should I expect the written report? Who do I contact if I have questions afterwards? What happens if ADHD is diagnosed, if it is not, or if the result is unclear? If medication is relevant, what happens next?
Worth doing Write your questions down beforehand rather than relying on memory. Long appointments are tiring, and questions are easy to lose.
