Before you start

This is your record, not a diagnostic form.

There are no right answers, nothing here is counted, and this worksheet does not calculate a score or produce a result. The choices are memory prompts rather than ADHD criteria, and ticking a lot of them means nothing in itself.

Record what you genuinely notice, including things that do not seem to fit ADHD. If you later have an assessment, your assessor will still need to reach their own conclusions.

You do not need to complete every section, or every question within a section. Use whatever helps you organise your thoughts and skip the rest.

What made me start wondering?

A change like that does not indicate ADHD in itself. It may alter the demands on you, affect how well existing coping strategies work, or simply make a longstanding pattern easier to notice.

03

Real-life examples

This is the part most worth spending time on. A specific example is far easier to describe — and far more useful later — than a general statement. One good example beats five half-finished ones, so stop whenever you have said what you wanted to say.

Only if another useful example comes to mind.

04

Areas I have noticed

Most people will find that only some areas apply. Skip anything that does not help. These are everyday descriptions gathered as memory prompts, not a list of symptoms, and there is no benefit in working through every card.

05

What I do to keep things working

Sometimes the strategies we use are as informative as the difficulty itself. An outcome may look effortless from the outside even when maintaining it takes considerable planning or pressure.

06

Looking back

ADHD is developmental, so an assessment usually explores whether relevant patterns were present earlier in life. You do not need perfect memories or old paperwork to use this section. Not having school reports, and not having anybody available who knew you as a child, does not automatically prevent an assessment — services differ, and it is worth asking what they accept.

07

What other people notice

Other people may notice things we miss, but their view is only one part of the picture. You do not have to involve anybody to use this worksheet, or to seek an assessment.

08

What else could be contributing?

Attention, memory, organisation, restlessness and emotional regulation can be affected by many things, and more than one may be present at once. Tick anything you think is worth mentioning. You are not being asked to decide whether it applies, only to flag it.

09

Things that do not fit, or make me unsure

This is not a section for talking yourself out of anything. It is simply the part of the picture that is easiest to leave out, and can be particularly useful to discuss with an assessor.

10

What would I actually like help with?

If you sought professional help, what would you most like to understand or improve?
